# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Readers who find marshall through posts shared in dev/PM circles (LinkedIn, Slack communities, RSS). They arrive at a single post, decide in seconds whether the voice is worth following, and maybe click through to kernels, talks, or the about page. Secondary: collaborators and hiring-adjacent visitors checking who marshall is.

## Product Purpose

marshallhouston.wtf is marshall's personal writing site: posts, one-sentence "kernels", talks, influences, and small interactive tools. It exists to ship thinking in public. Success is a reader finishing a post, feeling the specific voice, and subscribing (RSS) or returning.

## Positioning

Exploratory thinking shipped raw, in a lowercase voice no template could produce. The site is the proof of the BFF (build, friction, fix) philosophy it writes about.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: read another post (related posts, tags). Secondary: RSS subscribe.
- The line a visitor remembers: "this person actually builds and thinks out loud."
- Belief ladder: the voice is real -> the thinking is useful -> I want more of it.
- Proof on hand: the posts themselves, CultureCon talks, the cosmic-farmland repo.

## Brand Personality

Lowercase, direct, playful-serious. Fragments over complete sentences. The cap-toggle ("Aa" button that rewrites the page into Title Case) is the brand in miniature: a joke with an engineering commitment behind it. Dark, quiet, text-first; the personality lives in the words and the toggles, not in decoration.

## Anti-references

- LinkedIn-polish personal brands (gradient heroes, testimonial walls).
- AI-generic portfolio templates (cream backgrounds, serif-italic editorial affectation, eyebrow labels on every section).
- Anything that capitalizes the voice by default.

## Design Principles

- The words are the interface. Chrome stays quiet; typography and reading comfort carry everything.
- Jokes get engineering. Whimsy (cap-toggle, "lowerchaos") ships with real code, accessibility, and persistence.
- Ported, not templated. The minimal-mistakes dark skin is inherited identity; evolve it, don't replace it.
- Ship the thinking. Drafts, kernels, and rough edges are features; the site never gates on polish.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG target stated. Dark theme with high-contrast text. Inclusive-language pre-commit hook enforced on content. Keyboard and reduced-motion support expected as baseline.
